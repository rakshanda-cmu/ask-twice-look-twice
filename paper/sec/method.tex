\section{The diagnosis prescribes the prompt}
\label{sec:method}

Section~\ref{sec:anatomy} says the answer position under \sti{} cannot read the
question, and answers through image tokens instead. The repair follows without
design freedom: put something after the image that the answer position can read.
There are exactly two things available, and we use both.

\paragraph{Question echoing (\stit{}).} Repeat the question after the image. This
restores a question span adjacent to the answer position while leaving the
steered image tokens untouched, which is not merely plausible but forced:
$\cos(\sti{}, \stit{}) = 0.99999$ (Section~\ref{sec:anatomy-steering}), so
\stit{} repairs read-out and provably changes nothing about perception. It costs
$13$ tokens.

\paragraph{Image echoing (\sitit{}).} Repeat the image after the question, so the
answer position has both a nearby question and image tokens encoded with the
question already in context. Section~\ref{sec:ablation-cost} prices it.

The same construct serves two jobs, deliberately. As a diagnostic, \stit{} is the
controlled intervention that isolates read-out: matched on content, matched on
perception, differing only in whether the answer position has a question to read.
As a method, it is the cheaper of the two prompts we recommend. We did not pick
these orderings because they worked; they are what the mechanism leaves
available.

\subsection{Testing the prescription: the echo's content barely matters}
\label{sec:method-reversal}

If the echoed image re-anchors read-out rather than being re-perceived, damaging
its content should cost little. Reversing the second image copy (\sititrev{})
destroys the spatial correspondence between echoed patches and the scene while
keeping token count and position identical, and it is free within noise:
NaturalBench group accuracy moves from $37.37$ to $37.37$ ($p = 1.00$),
Winoground from $40.25$ to $41.00$ ($p = 0.68$), POPE F1 from $88.12$ to $88.25$,
RF20 F1 from $80.56$ to $79.78$. A second look that has been turned upside down
works as well as a correct one. Section~\ref{sec:ablation-resolution} makes the
same point on the other axis.

\begin{finding}
\textbf{Finding.} The echo works by position, not by content. Reversing the
echoed image copy changes nothing measurable (NaturalBench $\Delta = 0.000$,
$p = 1.00$; Winoground $\Delta = +0.007$, $p = 0.68$), and an
eighth-resolution echo matches a full one.
\end{finding}

\subsection{What the repair is worth, and what it is not}
\label{sec:method-honest}

Two results keep us from overclaiming, and we state them here rather than in a
closing limitations paragraph. First, question echoing alone is not reliably
better than simply asking the question last: on NaturalBench \stit{} scores
$35.00$ against \sit{}'s $35.05$ ($p = 1.00$). It is significantly better on
Winoground ($+0.057$, $p = 0.012$) and for Gemma-3-27B on NaturalBench
($+0.026$, $p = 0.014$), but the honest claim is that \stit{} repairs
question-first prompting rather than improving on question-last prompting.

Second, image echoing is a real gain over question-last, and this is the claim we
defend: $+0.085$ group accuracy on Winoground for Qwen3-VL-8B
($p = 2.4\times10^{-5}$), $+0.110$ for Gemma-3-27B ($p = 8.1\times10^{-7}$),
$+0.024$ on NaturalBench over \stit{} ($p = 0.016$), and $+1.18$ points averaged
over the 13 benchmarks of Section~\ref{sec:results}. On POPE it does not help:
question-last is already the best ordering there ($88.35$ F1 against \sitit{}'s
$88.12$), and we report that rather than omitting the benchmark.
