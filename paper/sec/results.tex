\section{Results: the repair holds across 13 benchmarks and 3 models}
\label{sec:results}

Section~\ref{sec:paradox} established the paradox on the four benchmarks where we
can also run the mechanistic analysis. Here we ask whether it is a quirk of those
four, and whether the repair survives contact with tasks the diagnosis was not
built on. We run the full ordering ladder on 13 further benchmarks spanning
single-image perception, high-resolution perception, counting, medical VQA and
four video QA datasets (Table~\ref{tab:breadth}, Appendix~\ref{app:breadth}).

\subsection{The paradox is large, general, and not a scoring artifact}

Moving the question from after the image to before it costs Qwen3-VL-8B $7.01$
points on average and hurts on 12 of 13 benchmarks. The effect is far larger than
the differences that usually separate published models: CV-Bench loses $18.01$
points, MMStar $11.40$, MMVP $9.00$, NExT-QA $8.91$. Nothing about the content of
the prompt changed. The system message, the question wording, the image, the
decoding parameters and the scoring code are identical across columns, and
\sti{} and \sit{} contain the same tokens in the same quantity. Only the position
of the question moved. WorldMedQA-V is the single benchmark where question-first
is not worse ($+1.06$); we report it rather than dropping it, and note it is also
the smallest benchmark in the set ($N = 568$).

\begin{finding}
\textbf{Finding.} The question-first paradox is general, not a property of one
benchmark. Across 13 benchmarks, moving the question before the image costs
Qwen3-VL-8B $7.01$ points on average and hurts on 12 of 13, with a worst case of
$18.01$ points on CV-Bench, while prompt content is held identical.
\end{finding}

\subsection{Thirteen tokens buy back most of the loss}

The two echo orderings separate the cost of the repair from its size. Echoing
the question after the image (\stit{}) recovers $5.49$ of the $7.01$ points, or
$78\%$ of the deficit, for $13$ extra tokens. Echoing the image after the
question (\sitit{}) recovers all of it and then exceeds the question-last
baseline by $1.18$ points, winning on 9 of 13 benchmarks.

The four video QA datasets are where \sitit{} is weakest (MSVD-QA $-0.78$,
NExT-QA $-0.26$, TGIF-QA $-1.06$ against \sit{}). These are the datasets where
the ``image'' is a frame sequence, so the echo doubles an already long visual
context, and TGIF-QA and MSVD-QA also have the lowest absolute accuracies in the
set. We do not claim image echoing as the right choice for video.

\begin{finding}
\textbf{Finding.} The repair is cheap in proportion to its size. Repeating only
the question after the image recovers $78\%$ of the question-first deficit
($+5.49$ of $7.01$ points) for $13$ tokens, and repeating the image recovers all
of it and adds $1.18$ points over question-last, winning on 9 of 13 benchmarks.
\end{finding}

\subsection{Cross-model scope: the paradox is model-specific, the repair is not}
\label{sec:crossmodel}

Running the same ladder on Gemma-3-27B and Gemma-4-31B separates two claims that
would otherwise be easy to conflate (Table~\ref{tab:crossmodel},
Appendix~\ref{app:models}). The paradox itself does not replicate on either
Gemma model: question-first is on average $0.14$ points \emph{better} than
question-last for Gemma-3-27B and $1.00$ points better for Gemma-4-31B. Whatever
makes question-first costly in Qwen3-VL-8B is not a universal property of
decoder-only vision-language models, and we do not claim it is.

Image echoing survives this transfer anyway. \sitit{} is the best of the four
orderings on 4 of 4 benchmarks for Gemma-4-31B and 3 of 4 for Gemma-3-27B, with
mean gains over question-last of $2.28$ and $1.66$ points. The intervention has a
wider domain than the diagnosis that motivated it, which is the honest way to
state its scope: we explain why it helps Qwen3-VL-8B, and we report that it also
helps two models whose read-out is not visibly broken.

\begin{finding}
\textbf{Finding.} The paradox and the repair have different scopes. Question-first
is not harmful on Gemma-3-27B ($+0.14$) or Gemma-4-31B ($+1.00$), so the paradox
is model-specific, yet image echoing is still the best ordering on 7 of 8
Gemma model-benchmark pairs (mean $+1.66$ and $+2.28$ over question-last).
\end{finding}
