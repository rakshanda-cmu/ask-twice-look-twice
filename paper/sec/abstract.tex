\begin{abstract}
Whether a question is placed before or after an image changes nothing about a
prompt's content, yet it changes a vision-language model's accuracy by up to
$18$ points. Question-first prompting, the layout that lets every image token
attend to the question, costs Qwen3-VL-8B $7.0$ points on average across 13
benchmarks and drives LLaVA-1.5 to exactly zero group accuracy. Degraded
perception does not explain this: question-first makes $2.49\times$ \emph{more}
image patches decode a content word from the question, and it measurably
rewrites the image tokens themselves. The ordering that looks most like it found
the answer is the one that answers wrong. We locate the failure in read-out. At
the peak read-out layer, question-first sends $2.19\times$ less attention from
the answer position to the question, the two orderings are indistinguishable at
chance through layer 25 of 36, and they then diverge to $P(\text{correct})$ of
$0.0986$ versus $0.8747$. Attention knockout makes the dependence causal and
doubly dissociated: severing answer-to-question costs question-last $5.6$ points
and question-first nothing, while severing answer-to-image costs question-first
$9.6$ points and question-last nothing. The diagnosis prescribes the fix.
Echoing the question after the image costs $13$ tokens and recovers $78\%$ of
the deficit while provably leaving perception untouched; echoing the image
recovers all of it and adds $1.18$ points over question-last. The echo works by
position, not content: a spatially reversed echo is statistically
indistinguishable from a correct one, and an eighth-resolution echo carrying
$\tfrac{1}{64}$ of the pixels matches a full one at $3\%$ of the added tokens.
\end{abstract}
